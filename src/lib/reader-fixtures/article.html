Swallows of the barn

The barn swallow is a bird that has crossed oceans for as long as anyone has kept records, and every spring the same pairs return to the same rafters to rebuild their mud cups. Watching them work is a lesson in patience, because each cup takes hundreds of trips and thousands of beakfuls of damp earth.

Researchers who band these birds report that they are remarkably faithful to their nesting sites, often returning within a few metres of the previous year's nest. That fidelity means a single well-placed beam can host a family line for decades, and many farmers consider the birds a sign of a healthy barn.

Their diet is almost entirely flying insects, caught on the wing in long looping sweeps over fields and water. A single adult can eat several hundred insects in a day, which makes them one of the quiet allies of anyone who grows food or keeps animals nearby.

In late summer the young gather on wires in noisy rows while the adults teach them to hunt. Within weeks the whole flock lifts and heads south, and the barn goes silent until the next spring brings the first scouts back to check the old rafters.

Where they nest

  • Rafters
  • Porches
nest = mud + grass
